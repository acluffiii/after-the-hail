Hail-repair cost estimator

Enter dent counts by size and the panels that were hit. You will get a rough paintless dent repair range, built only from published figures we opened, not from a made-up price list.

This is an educational estimate, not a quote. It is not an insurance settlement, a shop bid, or a promise of what anyone will charge or pay. Insurers and shops use their own matrices, labor rates, and judgments about access, paint condition, and whether a dent is even a PDR job. The low end follows a published insurer program rate sheet, which is what participating shops in that program agreed to bill the insurer — not a retail menu. Hidden dents, prep, and retail work often cost more. If a panel is off that sheet, we do not invent a number.

How the range is calculated

Hail PDR is usually priced per panel from a matrix: dent count in a quantity bracket, then the predominant coin size. We opened a complete published matrix and several shop-published retail guides. Because those sources disagree, and because none of them is “the” market price, the tool reports a range and shows its work.

  1. Low end. For each panel you enter, we look up the price in State Farm’s PDR Pricing Matrix for its Paintless Dent Repair Agreement, form dated 29 April 2025, which we opened at the public State Farm URL cited below. Brackets are 1–5, 6–15, 16–30, 31–50, and, where the sheet lists them, 51–75 through 201–250. Hood, roof, and deck lid have the later brackets; many other panels do not. A blank cell, or a cell marked MCE (“most cost effective”), is treated as off-matrix: we do not invent a low-end price. Aluminum, or a full-size van, full-size SUV, or extended-cab truck roof, adds 25%, and the sheet allows a maximum of one of those 25% upcharges per panel. Dents larger than a half-dollar add $50 each, from the same sheet. R&I (removing and installing trim or parts) and corrosion protection are not included in the low end, even though the sheet lists them separately.
  2. High end. Eco Auto Body, a Colorado shop that published a consumer explanation of hail matrices, says visible counts often miss dents and tells readers to move their estimate up one or two quantity brackets, and to add about 20%, plus 20–40% for typical prep work. This tool moves up one bracket and applies the top of that prep range (40%) to form the high end. If that next bracket is not listed, the high end uses National Dent Repair’s published hail maximum of $2,200 per panel instead of a made-up cell. The high end is also at least $195 per included panel (National Dent Repair’s published hail starting figure) and is capped at $2,200 per panel. The same 25% and $50 rules are applied when they apply.
  3. Whole-vehicle context only. After the panels are added up, we also point to T P Auto Repair’s 2025 guide, which describes typical whole-vehicle hail work as $1,000–$3,000 for light to moderate damage and $3,000–$5,000 or more for heavy damage. That comparison is not part of the arithmetic.

Limits of this method: the State Farm sheet is an insurer program rate, not what a cash customer is quoted, and State Farm’s Select Service page describes the form as proprietary. We link the official PDF rather than republishing the full grid. Eco Auto Body’s examples and National Dent Repair’s $195–$2,200 per-panel hail range are one shop’s published figures, not a statewide survey. T P Auto Repair says aluminum often adds about 10–20%, while the State Farm sheet, National Dent Repair, and an IA Path industry note all use 25%; the calculator uses 25% and notes the disagreement. Your car, paint, access, and local labor will differ.

Sources we opened

  1. State Farm, PDR Pricing Matrix (PDF, form footer 1003111 125278.13, dated 29 April 2025). Per-panel prices, quantity brackets, majority size, aluminum and tall-roof 25% upcharges (maximum one per panel), $50 oversize dents, R&I, and MCE cells. This is the low-end lookup table.
  2. State Farm, Paintless Dent Repair Select Service. Participating shops agree to use that matrix; the page also describes the form as proprietary information.
  3. Eco Auto Body, “How Much Does PDR Cost for Hail Damage?” (Wheat Ridge / Federal Heights, Colorado). Matrix structure; example of 1–5 dime-size fender dents at $75–$125 plus about $40 prep; example of 51–75 dime-size roof dents at $250–$375 plus about $200 prep; advice to move up one or two brackets for hidden dents and to add 20%; prep typically adds 20–40%.
  4. National Dent Repair, dent repair cost guide (page opened; it was dated 14 November 2024). Hail damage starting at $195 per panel and up to $2,200 per panel; aluminum +25%.
  5. T P Auto Repair, “How Much Does Paintless Dent Repair Cost?” (dated 1 October 2025). Localized hail on a panel $150–$400; a whole vehicle commonly $1,000–$3,000 for light to moderate hail and $3,000–$5,000 or more when damage is heavier; aluminum typically 10–20% more.
  6. IA Path, “The PDR Technician’s Guide to Understanding Insurance Billing”. Majority-size rule; aluminum or HSS +25%; double metal +25%; tall or SUV or extended-cab roof +25%; oversized dents described at $40 (the State Farm sheet we opened lists $50; the calculator follows the State Farm figure for the low end).

Questions

Is this what my insurer or a shop will charge?
No. This is an educational estimate, not a quote and not a settlement. Insurers and shops use their own matrices, and they may decide a dent is not a paintless repair at all.
Where do the numbers come from?
The low end of each range follows the per-panel prices in State Farm’s publicly posted PDR Pricing Matrix dated April 29, 2025, which is an insurer program rate sheet, not a retail menu. The high end applies published shop guidance: move up one quantity bracket and add 20–40 percent for typical prep and hidden dents, then compare that with published retail panel ranges.
What if my car has more dents than the matrix lists?
We do not invent a price for off-matrix or “most cost effective” cells. The page will say so and point you to published shop-guide ceilings and a written estimate from a shop.
